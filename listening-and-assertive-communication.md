# Listening and Assertive Communication

## Question 1: What are the steps to do Active Listening?

1. Focus on the speaker and keep my own thoughts aside for some time.
2. Wait and do not cut the speaker in the middle of a sentence.
3. Encourage the speaker with short phrases such as "go on" or "I see", which are called door openers.
4. Use body language that shows interest, like looking at the speaker, nodding and sitting straight.
5. Say the main idea again in different words, to check if I understood correctly.
6. Ask a question if something is not clear.
7. Note down the important points when the topic is serious or technical.

## Question 2: Key points of Reflective Listening in Fisher's model

* The listener should speak less and listen more.
* The listener should respond to the feelings and the personal meaning of the message, not to the dry facts only.
* Try to see the matter the way the speaker sees it.
* Repeat or restate what the speaker said so that they can say yes or correct it.
* Avoid giving opinions, advice or arguments in the beginning.
* Watch the body language and tone, because they also carry the message.
* Remove distractions and stay present in the conversation.

## Question 3: What are the obstacles in my listening process?

* I lose focus when the conversation is long or when I am looking at my laptop or phone.
* I start to plan my answer while the other person is still speaking.
* I get impatient when the speaker takes too long to reach the point.
* I judge the topic as boring or already known, and then I do not listen fully.
* Bad internet or noise during online calls.

## Question 4: What can I do to improve my listening?

* Close other tabs and keep the phone aside during meetings and calls.
* Take small notes, which also keeps me involved.
* Stop myself from interrupting, and wait for the speaker to complete the point.
* Repeat the important points at the end to confirm them.
* Ask follow-up questions instead of assuming.
* Practise listening in daily talks with friends and family, not only in meetings.

## Question 5: When do I switch to Passive communication?

I do this when I do not want to disturb the mood of a group or when everyone else seems to agree on something. I stay quiet even if I have a different opinion. It also happens when I am not sure about my knowledge of a topic and I fear that I may say something wrong in front of seniors.

## Question 6: When do I switch to Aggressive communication?

Usually when I feel that I am not being heard, or when a mistake is blamed on me unfairly. Then my voice goes up and I interrupt people. It also happens when I am very tired or when many things go wrong on the same day.

## Question 7: When do I switch to Passive Aggressive communication?

I do it when I am annoyed but I do not want to start an argument. For example, I may taunt someone lightly, give a sarcastic reply, take a long time to answer their message, or talk about it with another friend instead of the person. These things do not solve anything, and I know it.

## Question 8: How can I make my communication assertive?

* Tell the other person what the problem is and how I feel, without attacking them. For example, "I feel stressed when the plan changes at the last moment."
* Be direct about what I want, in a polite way and with simple words.
* Learn to say no to extra work when my plate is already full, and offer another option when possible.
* Keep a calm voice and look at the person while talking.
* Take a deep breath and a few seconds before I answer in a heated moment.
* Listen to the other person's side also, because assertive does not mean getting my own way every time.
* Talk about the problem on the same day, so that it does not turn into taunts or silence later.
