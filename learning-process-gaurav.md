# Learning Process

## Question 1: What is the Feynman Technique? Explain in 1 line.

The Feynman Technique means to write or say a topic in easy words, as if I am teaching a beginner, and to study again the points that I could not explain.

## Question 2: In the Barbara Oakley video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

The most interesting idea for me was how famous people used their rest time to solve problems. In the talk, the stories of Salvador Dali and Thomas Edison are told. They would sit in a chair holding an object like a key or a metal ball. When they started to fall asleep, the object dropped, made a sound and woke them up. In that half-sleep state, they got new ideas.

I liked this because it shows that our brain is working even when we are not sitting with a book. Many times I keep staring at a problem for hours and get nothing. After this video, I think it is better to take a short break and come back with a fresh mind. The idea is simple, but I never thought about learning in this way before.

## Question 3: What are active and diffused modes of thinking?

* Active mode: In this mode, our mind is fully focused on one task. It is the mode of attention. We use it when we practise a problem or read something new with full concentration.
* Diffused mode: In this mode, our mind is free and relaxed, and it wanders. It happens when we take a walk, listen to music, rest or sleep. The brain makes links between different ideas without our effort.

The video explains that we cannot use both modes at the same time. Learning works best when we go to the active mode first, and then give the brain some time in the diffused mode.

## Question 4: According to the video, what are the steps to take when approaching a new topic?

1. Decide exactly what we want to learn.
2. Divide the skill into small pieces.
3. Choose the most important pieces and start with them.
4. Collect a few good resources, but do not keep reading forever.
5. Remove the things that distract us.
6. Practise for a minimum of 20 hours.

## Question 5: What are some of the actions I can take going forward to improve my learning process?

* I will study in short rounds of focused work followed by a short break, and I will walk or rest for a few minutes in the break instead of using social media.
* After learning a topic, I will try to teach it to a friend or write it in my own words. If I cannot do it, I will go back to the topic.
* I will not copy code from anywhere without understanding it. I will write the code myself and try to change it in small ways to see what happens.
* I will keep my phone on silent and out of my reach during work hours, and I will use only work-related notifications.
* I will make a small time plan for each topic with a deadline, because the deadline helps me to use the time properly.
* When I get stuck, I will first try on my own for some time. If I am still stuck, I will clearly tell my mentor what I tried and where the problem is.
* I will revise the old topics once every week, so that I do not forget them.
* I will sleep for enough hours, because tired brains learn very slowly.
