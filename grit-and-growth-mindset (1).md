# Grit and Growth Mindset

## Question 1: Paraphrase the Grit video

The video tells us that grit is the habit of sticking to a goal for many years with interest and hard work, and not leaving it when things get difficult. It says that people who keep going after failures usually do better than people who only depend on natural talent.

## Question 2: Paraphrase the Introduction to Growth Mindset video

The video compares two ways of thinking. In a fixed mindset, a person believes that their ability is fixed and fears failure. In a growth mindset, a person believes that the brain and skills grow with practice, so they enjoy learning, accept mistakes and keep trying.

## Question 3: What is the Internal Locus of Control? What is the key point in the video?

Locus of control tells us where we think the control of our life lies. If it is internal, I feel that the results in my life depend on my own effort and decisions. If it is external, I feel that luck, other people or situations decide everything.

The key point is that an internal locus of control is the secret of staying motivated. If we believe that our effort matters, we keep working. If we believe that nothing we do matters, we stop trying. The video suggests that we can develop the internal locus by solving our own problems and giving ourselves credit for the actions that brought the result.

## Question 4: Key points to build a growth mindset

1. Have faith that I can figure things out.
2. Challenge my own assumptions.
3. Create a life curriculum of my own, with the skills I want to build.
4. Respect the struggle and do not run away from it.

## Question 5: My ideas to take action and build a growth mindset

* I will add the word "yet" to my sentences, like "I do not understand recursion yet".
* I will pick one hard topic every week and spend some focused time on it, even if it feels uncomfortable.
* When my code fails, I will read the error message calmly and try to fix it before I ask for help.
* I will ask for feedback after every assignment and write down two things that I can improve.
* I will celebrate my effort and small wins, such as solving a problem which I could not solve last week.
* I will keep a small notebook or file for my mistakes and the lessons from them.
* I will limit negative self-talk. Instead of "I am bad at this", I will say "I need more practice at this".
* I will learn from people who are ahead of me, and take their success as a motivation and not as a threat.
* I will be consistent. A little progress every day is better than studying a lot for one day and then stopping.
