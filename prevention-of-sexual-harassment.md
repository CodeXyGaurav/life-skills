# Prevention of Sexual Harassment

## What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment means behaviour of a sexual kind that the other person does not welcome. The intention of the person does not decide it. The effect on the other person does. Some examples:

* Touching, patting, hugging or brushing against someone without permission.
* Jokes, comments, nicknames or remarks that are sexual or about someone's body.
* Looking at a person again and again in a way that makes them uncomfortable.
* Messages, emails, photos, videos or memes that are sexual in nature.
* Repeated requests for dates or meetings after the person has said no.
* Asking for sexual favours in exchange for work benefits, or threatening someone's job.
* Following the person, or checking on them in a way that scares them.
* Creating a hostile workplace, where a person feels afraid or embarrassed to come to work.

It can happen face to face, over chat or calls, and outside the office during work events or trips.

## What would I do if I face or witness such behaviour?

If I face it:

1. Say clearly and firmly that I do not like the behaviour and that it should stop, if I feel safe.
2. Keep proof, which means screenshots, messages and a diary of dates and events.
3. Make a complaint to the Internal Committee or HR, following the company PoSH policy.
4. Talk to someone I trust for support so that I do not carry it alone.

If I witness it:

1. Safely interrupt the situation, for example by calling the person away or by saying that the behaviour is not okay.
2. Meet the affected person later, ask if they are fine and tell them about the reporting options.
3. Report what I saw to the proper committee, and keep the details private.
4. Not stay silent or laugh along, because that makes the behaviour look acceptable.

If it is repeated, I will report it without waiting, since repeated behaviour usually gets worse.
